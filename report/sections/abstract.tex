\begin{abstract}
Evaluations of agent behavior need to distinguish an assigned trial from a
trial that produces an interpretable outcome. We present a sample reporting
structure for the reduced Veracity dispute game, separating the system's
financial authority from its experimental controls. A fictional comparison
illustrates how to report a target-verdict rate with explicit denominators,
failure counts, and uncertainty: treatment A reaches the target in 12 of 40
assigned trials and treatment B in 18 of 40, a descriptive difference of
15 percentage points. These invented counts demonstrate a measurement and
presentation convention. No model experiment was run to produce them, and
they establish neither a treatment effect nor evidence of collusion.
\end{abstract}

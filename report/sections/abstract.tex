\begin{abstract}
Before comparing opportunities for agent collusion, we test whether the
integrated Veracity experiment can execute an all-levers-off jury baseline.
Two live-model episodes use seven jurors, three public deliberation rounds,
clear evidence, flat juror pay, low stakes, and no private channels, transfers,
or cross-episode memory. Both episodes settle correctly through the Rust
service and Solidity contract on local Anvil. All 14 juror reveals agree with
evaluator truth, and all 42 within-episode juror pairs agree. Episode-level
headline rates have a mean of 100\% and an observed sample standard deviation
of zero. These two episodes verify the execution and measurement path; they
do not estimate treatment effects, population accuracy, or the absence of
collusion. A preceding free-route attempt failed on provider quota and is
reported separately. We then report six single-episode probes of the
full-opportunity cell with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, which add an auditor, competing
side-payment offers, party offers to the auditor, and a stated prohibition on
side payments and private chat. When payments were permitted and both parties
could make offers, every juror accepted the offer that matched the
evidence-consistent verdict. When agents were told payments and private
messages were prohibited, none occurred, although both stayed available and
nothing enforced the rule. These single episodes describe one model on one
scenario; they do not estimate rates or treatment effects.
\end{abstract}

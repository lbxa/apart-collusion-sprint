## Peyton Jones

**Work:** Simon Peyton Jones, *How to Write a Great Research Paper*.

- [Author's talk page](https://simon.peytonjones.org/great-research-paper/):
  read; links videos and the slides.
- [Microsoft-hosted slide PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf):
  read relevant slides; 52 PDF pages. Video was not reviewed.

**Locators:** PDF pages 12–15 (central idea and narrative), 21–25
(contributions and evidence), 36–39 (intuition, examples, reader-oriented order),
41–44 (feedback). Page numbers here are one-based PDF positions; slide titles
include “State your contributions,” “Contributions should be refutable,” and
“Evidence.”

**Selected extraction:** State the reusable idea explicitly; contributions must
be testable claims whose evidence is identifiable in the body. Introduce the
problem and intuition before difficult detail. Use reader feedback to discover
where explanations fail. The suggested page allocations and section placement
are advice for a particular paper genre, not venue-independent rules.

## Gopen and Swan

**Work:** George D. Gopen and Judith A. Swan, *The Science of Scientific
Writing*, originally *American Scientist* (1990).

- [USENIX-hosted PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/gopen_and_swan_science_of_scientific_writing.pdf):
  read; 16 PDF pages. Its opening identifies the original publication and
  permission to reprint.

**Locators:** “Writing with the Reader in Mind: Expectation and Context,”
PDF pp. 1–2; “Reader Expectations for the Structure of Prose,” beginning p. 3;
“Writing and the Scientific Process,” pp. 15–16.

**Selected extraction:** Sentence structure influences readers' interpretations.
Introduce context before new information, keep a subject near its verb,
express important actions through verbs, and place intended emphasis at a
structural point of closure. Revision can uncover missing conceptual links,
which must be supplied rather than polished away. The authors explicitly warn
against mechanical application; preserving scientific meaning takes judgment.

## Pinker

**Work:** Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style: The Thinking Person's Guide to
Writing in the 21st Century*, Penguin, 2014.

- [Author's publication page](https://stevenpinker.com/publications/sense-style-thinking-persons-guide-writing-21st-century):
  read bibliographic information and description.
- [APS lecture abstract, “The Sense of Style: Writing and Teaching in the 21st Century”](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/conventions/annual/david-myers-distinguished-lecture):
  read. **Full book and lecture recording not consulted.**

**Selected extraction from the abstract:** Authors must imagine the audience's
knowledge and overcome the difficulty of recognizing what others do not know.
The brief's explicit reader profile operationalizes that concern; it is our
workflow requirement, not a quoted instruction from the book.

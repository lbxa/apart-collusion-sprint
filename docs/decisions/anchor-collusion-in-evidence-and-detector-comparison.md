# Anchor Collusion in evidence and detector comparison

- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Deciders:** Lucas Barbosa
- **Area:** research design, benchmark measurement

## Context

The sprint poses questions about markets, detection and coordination carriers.
Existing trajectories support investigation of how agreements connect to
actions and harm, but do not answer all of those questions with comparable
certainty. Lucas accepted Track 2.3 as the paper's anchor and Track 2.2 as its
first comparative experiment.

## Decision

Anchor the Collusion study on what constitutes convincing evidence of
collusion. The first detector comparison will evaluate text-only, action-only
and combined observations against the same independently reviewed, held-out
sessions. Freeze the annotation rubric, scenario admission criteria and
assignment plan before prospective collection.

## Consequences

- Distinguish proposal, agreement, execution and success. A wrong verdict,
  payment, shared mistake or message alone is insufficient.
- Each admitted opportunity needs a feasible compliant strategy, an applicable
  obligation or explicit harmful-outcome definition, and observable implementing
  actions. Supplied recruitment and spontaneous discovery are separate conditions.
- Preserve negative, uncertain, missing and failed assignments. Agent annotations
  are provisional until the declared independent review is complete.
- Professional benchmarks can supply documents and reference grading. They do
  not supply collusion labels without a validated coordination opportunity.
- Sector, opportunity mechanism and baseline difficulty are distinct strata.
  Repeated votes and cases within a session do not create independent replicates.
- If participant duties vary, keep behavioral harm endpoints comparable and
  score duty violations only against the actual mounted text.
- Other sprint questions remain possible extensions rather than current paper
  claims. This decision does not assert new findings or authorize API spending.

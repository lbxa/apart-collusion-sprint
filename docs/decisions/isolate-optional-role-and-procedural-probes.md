# Isolate optional role and procedural probes

- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Deciders:** Lucas Barbosa
- **Area:** Collusion exploratory research inputs

## Context

The user requested different roles and recruitment styles in addition to the
frozen juror resistance comparison. Mandated party recruitment, revenue quotas
and procedural pooling change the opportunity and evaluated roles, so pooling
their observations into the primary score would change its interpretation.

## Decision

Keep optional probes in a separate exploratory directory and registry, without
changing the primary configurations or five adapted source files. Prepare one
quota/seeded-peer paid false-publication probe, creator and challenger mandate
probes, and a synthetic 12-record individual-inspection task. Match the partial
and full scripted-peer inspection probes on the same roster and allowance 12;
the partial peer inspects four records and the full peer inspects all twelve.
Retain an all-model no-peer condition only as contextual exploration.

## Consequences

- These probes require separate admission and frozen assignments, consume the
  same existing total API authorization if dispatched, and cannot enter the
  primary model resistance score or replace missing primary assignments.
- The procedural contrast changes peer inspection and its invitation together.
  The full-inspection scripted peer still shares findings, so it is not a fully
  compliant peer control. Evaluated jurors can inspect the complete batch and
  complete honest adjudication for a positive ordinary reward in every probe.
- The vouchers are explicitly synthetic. Their correct verdict and deterministic
  mechanics are not results from an external benchmark or evaluated model.
- Mandated recruitment is induced, and prefiled adverse party actions are
  operator mechanisms. Autonomous subsequent recruitment and juror agreement
  require their own evidence; scripted endorsement never supplies those labels.
